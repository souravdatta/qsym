# Part 12 — BB84: Cryptography Where Eavesdropping Leaves Fingerprints

*Quantum Computing with qsym, a series for developers. Full code:
[github.com/souravdatta/qsym](https://github.com/souravdatta/qsym)*

Every key exchange you use today (Diffie–Hellman, RSA) is safe because some
math problem is *believed* hard. BB84 — invented by Bennett and Brassard in
1984 — is different: its security rests on physics. Measuring a qubit
disturbs it (chapter 6), and unknown qubits can't be copied (chapter 3). So
an eavesdropper **cannot listen without leaving evidence**. Let's build the
whole protocol, then play the eavesdropper and watch her get caught.

## The protocol

Alice sends Bob a stream of single qubits. For each one she picks a random
**bit** and a random **basis**:

- **Z basis**: bit 0 → |0⟩, bit 1 → |1⟩
- **X basis**: bit 0 → |+⟩, bit 1 → |−⟩ (the H-rotated versions)

Bob, knowing neither, measures each qubit in his own random basis. Then, over
a public channel, they reveal only their *basis* choices (never the bits) and
**sift**: keep the ~50% of positions where the bases matched. There, Bob's
result equals Alice's bit — a shared secret key. Where they mismatched, the
result was a coin flip; discard.

The punchline comes last: they sacrifice a sample of key bits, compare them
publicly, and check the error rate.

## The code

Each qubit is its own tiny one-qubit simulation:

```racket
#lang racket
(require qsym)

(define (random-bit rng) (if (< (random rng) 0.5) 0 1))

;; Alice: encode a bit in a basis.
(define (alice-prepare bit basis)
  (define state (if (= bit 1) (gX q0) q0))
  (if (= basis 1) (gH state) state))          ; basis 1 = X basis

;; Measure in a basis: for the X basis, rotate with H first.
(define (measure-qubit state basis rng)
  (define st (if (= basis 1) (gH state) state))
  (define circ (make-circuit (list (measure 0)) #:qubits 1 #:clbits 1))
  (define-values (_ cbits) (run-shot circ st #:rng rng))
  (vector-ref cbits 0))

(define (bb84 n #:eve? [eve? #f] #:seed [seed 42])
  (define rng (make-pseudo-random-generator))
  (parameterize ([current-pseudo-random-generator rng]) (random-seed seed))
  (define alice-bits  (for/list ([_ n]) (random-bit rng)))
  (define alice-bases (for/list ([_ n]) (random-bit rng)))
  (define bob-bases   (for/list ([_ n]) (random-bit rng)))
  ;; Transmission — possibly through Eve.
  (define bob-results
    (for/list ([bit alice-bits] [ab alice-bases] [bb bob-bases])
      (define sent (alice-prepare bit ab))
      (define arrived
        (if eve?
            ;; Intercept-resend: Eve measures in a random basis
            ;; and forwards a fresh qubit matching HER result.
            (let* ([eve-basis (random-bit rng)]
                   [stolen    (measure-qubit sent eve-basis rng)])
              (alice-prepare stolen eve-basis))
            sent))
      (measure-qubit arrived bb rng)))
  ;; Sift: keep positions where Alice's and Bob's bases matched.
  (define sifted
    (for/list ([ab alice-bases] [bb bob-bases]
               [abit alice-bits] [bbit bob-results]
               #:when (= ab bb))
      (cons abit bbit)))
  (define n-err (for/sum ([p sifted] #:when (not (= (car p) (cdr p)))) 1))
  (displayln (format "sifted key: ~a bits, errors: ~a (QBER ~a%)"
                     (length sifted) n-err
                     (real->decimal-string
                      (* 100.0 (/ n-err (max (length sifted) 1))) 1))))
```

## Run it — first clean, then bugged

```racket
(bb84 200)
; sifted key: 88 bits, errors: 0 (QBER 0.0%)

(bb84 200 #:eve? #t)
; sifted key: 88 bits, errors: 21 (QBER 23.9%)
```

Without Eve: 200 qubits sift down to an 88-bit key with **zero** errors —
Alice and Bob now share a secret usable as a one-time pad.

With Eve wiretapping every qubit: the sifted key is suddenly ~24% wrong.
The theory says exactly 25%: Eve guesses the wrong basis half the time, and
each wrong-basis measurement resends a disturbed qubit that flips Bob's
sifted bit half the time. ½ × ½ = 25%.

So Alice and Bob compare a random sample of their key over the public
channel. Expected mismatch ~0%? Channel is clean, use the rest as the key.
Mismatch near 25%? **Someone is on the line** — throw the key away and try
another channel. Eve's dilemma is total: measuring disturbs, and no-cloning
forbids keeping a copy to measure later. Eavesdropping is not just
detectable — it's *guaranteed* detectable, by physics.

This isn't science fiction, either: BB84 runs today over fiber and even
satellite links, and it's the one quantum protocol you can fully grasp with
nothing but chapters 1–6 of this series.
